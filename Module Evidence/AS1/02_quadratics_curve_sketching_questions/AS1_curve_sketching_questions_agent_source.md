## Solution to Question 2: C1 January 2011 Question 7

**Question ID:** AS1CurveSketchingQ002  
**Source:** Mark Scheme PDF p.3  
**Original reference:** C1 January 2011 Question 7  
**Related question:** See `AS1_curve_sketching_questions.md`, Question 2  
**Marks available:** 14  
**Match confidence:** High

### Official Mark Scheme Solution

| Step | Official working / result | Mark code | Mark awarded | Brief annotation |
|---:|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | $x(9-x^2)=0$ | M1 | 1 | Sets the expression equal to zero to find x-intercepts. |
| 2 | $x=0$ or $x=3$ or $x=-3$; hence $(0,0)$, $(3,0)$, $(-3,0)$ | W2 | 2 | Finds all three intercepts. |
| 3 | $\frac{dy}{dx}=9-3x^2$ | M1 W1 | 2 | Differentiates correctly. |
| 4 | $9-3x^2=0$ | M1 | 1 | Sets the derivative equal to zero. |
| 5 | $x=\sqrt{3}$ or $x=-\sqrt{3}$ | W1 | 1 | Finds the stationary x-values. |
| 6 | If $x=\sqrt{3}$, $y=6\sqrt{3}$ | M1 | 1 | Substitutes into the curve. |
| 7 | If $x=-\sqrt{3}$, $y=-6\sqrt{3}$ | W1 | 1 | Finds the second stationary y-value. |
| 8 | $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=-6x$ | M1 | 1 | Uses the second derivative to classify stationary points. |
| 9 | $(\sqrt{3},6\sqrt{3})$ is a maximum turning point | W1 | 1 | Correctly identifies the maximum. |
| 10 | $(-\sqrt{3},-6\sqrt{3})$ is a minimum turning point | W1 | 1 | Correctly identifies the minimum. |
| 11 | Sketch of $y=9x-x^3$ showing roots $-3$, $0$, $3$ and turning points at $(-\sqrt{3},-6\sqrt{3})$ and $(\sqrt{3},6\sqrt{3})$ | MW2 | 2 | Completes the sketch using the results found. |

### Final Answer

The curve crosses the x-axis at

$$
(-3,0),\quad (0,0),\quad (3,0).
$$

The stationary points are

$$
(-\sqrt{3},-6\sqrt{3})\ \text{minimum},
\quad
(\sqrt{3},6\sqrt{3})\ \text{maximum}.
$$

### What the Examiner Wanted

The examiner wanted the roots found from the factorised expression, the stationary points found using differentiation, and the nature of each point justified using the second derivative. The sketch had to use the intercepts and stationary points consistently.
